People

Principal Investigator

Prof. Lav Varshney

Director

Prof. Lav R. Varshney is the Della Pietra Infinity Professor and inaugural director of the AI Innovation Institute at Stony Brook University. He is co-founder and CEO of Kocree, Inc., a startup company building novel human-controllable AI for discovery and creativity, and chief scientist of Ensaras, Inc., a startup company focused on AI and wastewater treatment. He holds appointments at RAND Corporation and at Brookhaven National Laboratory. He was previously on the faculty of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, a visiting scholar at Northwestern's Kellogg School of Management, a principal research scientist at Salesforce Research AI, and a research staff member at IBM Research. He is a former White House staffer, having served on the National Security Council staff as a White House Fellow, where he contributed to national AI and wireless communications policy. His research interests include information and coding theory, statistical signal processing, artificial intelligence, neuroscience, and creativity. He received his B.S. degree from Cornell University and his S.M. and Ph.D. degrees from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.

Postdoctoral Researchers

AI Innovation Institute at Stony Brook University

style="width: 150px; height: auto; vertical-align: middle; margin-right: 12px;">Matthew Babbitt is a Postdoctoral Associate at the AI Innovation Institute. His mathematical interests intersect with Algebra, Algebraic Geometry, Galois Theory, and Elliptic Curves, while his AI research interests focus on theoretical foundations of Machine Learning as well as applications of LLMs to mathematical creation. His current research program seeks to clarify relationships between various algebras and information lattices for use in Information Lattice Learning. Matthew also writes math contest problems, holding memberships on multiple editorial boards. He is also a founding director on the board of the Upstate New York Math Team nonprofit organization dedicated to coaching high-school students for math contests. Matthew received a B.S. in mathematics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2018 and a Ph.D. in pure mathematics from the University of Texas at Dallas in 2025 through the mentorship of Carlos Arreche.

style="width: 150px; height: auto; vertical-align: middle; margin-right: 12px;">Babak Hemmatian received a degree in clinical psychology from the University of Tehran in his native Iran before moving to the United States, where he received an M.S. in Computer Science and a Ph.D. in Cognitive Science from Brown University. Previously, he was a Beckman Postdoctoral Fellow at the Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, and the Associate Director of the Center for Brain, Biology and Behavior at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln. He studies how complex reasoning evolves naturally in networks of agents, be they humans, machines, or a combination of the two. To this end, he combines theories from cognitive science and neuroscience with behavioral experiments on one hand, and advanced computational methods on the other. He has applied this integrative perspective to domains ranging from moral reasoning to general intelligence to argumentation on social media.

Graduate Students

Lily Zhang is a Ph.D. student in Applied Mathematics and Statistics at Stony Brook University. She holds a master’s degree from Harvard University, where she was named to the Dean’s List, and a bachelor’s degree from Wellesley College. She serves on the mathematics faculty at Hofstra University during the academic year and at Brown University during the summer. Her research lies at the intersection of topology and machine learning, with a focus on using topological methods to study higher order structure of population representations. Applications include neuroscience, condensed matter physics, and robotics. Previously, she worked as an engineer at Siemens and at a biomedical startup.

Current Collaborators

Yu Wen was a Postdoctoral Associate in the group at the AI Innovation Institute at Stony Brook University. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Engineering from the University of Houston, his M.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the University of Florida, and his B.S. in Computer Science and Technology from Huazhong University of Science and Technology. His research spans AI algorithms, applications, and systems, with interdisciplinary interests in computer architecture and computer graphics. He is now an Assistant Professor at the University of Houston.

Razan Baltaji is a graduate student in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. She received her B.S. and M.S. degrees in physics from the American University of Beirut. Her research interests lie at the intersection of science and machine learning as well as multimodal machine learning. She is also passionate about globalization of engineering research and education.

Anay Pattanaik is a graduate student in the Department of Computer Science. He received his B.Tech. degree in aerospace engineering from the Indian Institute of technology (IIT) Kanpur in 2016. His research interests include decision making under uncertainty and machine learning.

Vignesh Sundaresha is a graduate student in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. He previously worked at AMD as a Silicon Design Engineer. He obtained his B.Tech. and M.Tech. degrees in electrical engineering from the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Madras in 2021. His Master’s thesis on “Design of Quasi-Cyclic LDPC Decoder for 5G NR” was completed as part of a collaboration with EPFL. His current research interests involve designing efficient, explainable, and analyzable algorithms for computer vision tasks.

Nghia Dai Nguyen is a graduate student in the Department of Computer Science as a VinUni-Illinois Smart Health Center (VISHC) Fellow. He received his B.S. degree in computer science from VinUniversity in 2024. His research interests include smart health and causal inference.

Haizi Yu is a research associate in the Coordinated Science Laboratory and co-founder of Kocree, Inc. He completed his Ph.D. dissertation, “Automatic Concept Learning via Information Lattices,” in the group in 2019. He received his M.S. degree in computer science from Stanford University, and his B.S. degree from the Department of Automation at Tsinghua University. His research interests include machine learning, interpretable feature learning, automatic knowledge discovery, and music intelligence. He was also a postdoctoral scholar at the Knowledge Lab, University of Chicago.
